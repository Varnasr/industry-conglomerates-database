Findings · Cement · Part 1, Concentration

The Adani group built India's second largest cement business in four years, almost entirely by acquisition

The Adani family took control of Ambuja Cements and, through it, ACC from Holcim in September 2022. The group then bought Sanghi Industries (December 2023), Penna Cement (August 2024) and Orient Cement (April 2025). Consolidated capacity went from 67.5 million tonnes a year in March 2023 to 109 in March 2026.

UltraTech, the largest, had 196.8. Against the Economic Survey's figure of about 690 million tonnes for all of India, the two together hold roughly 44% of capacity and Adani about 16%. Those two shares are indicative only: the national figure has no stated date and UltraTech's includes plants abroad.

Cement is sold regionally, so national capacity shares say little about market power in any one region. The Competition Commission's orders on each acquisition would give regional shares; they could not be retrieved for this paper.

How this was measured

Consolidated capacity at 31 March from Ambuja Cements' and UltraTech's annual reports. Consolidation counts each subsidiary in full whatever the stake (Ambuja held 50.05% of ACC at acquisition, and about 73% of Orient by March 2026), so ownership-weighted capacity would be lower. Targets of 119 and 140 million tonnes in company material are plans and are not used.

Every figure above is in the data tables with its source and page. Read the argument in full in Part 1 of the paper.
