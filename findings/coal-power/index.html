Findings · Coal power · Part 1, Concentration

Adani Power runs 24% of India's private coal power capacity, mostly bought in a market that stopped growing

Private coal and lignite capacity grew fast to 2017-18 and then stopped: it was 75,546 MW in 2017-18 and 74,892 MW in 2025-26. Over the same years Adani Power went from 10,440 MW to 18,110 MW.

The share therefore traced a U. It was 18.9% in 2013-14, fell to 13.6% by 2018-19 while others built, and rose to 24.2% as Adani bought plants: Udupi in April 2015, Raipur and Raigarh in 2019-20, and Mahan in March 2022. Godda, 1,600 MW in Jharkhand, was built by the group and sells its power to Bangladesh.

Of all coal and lignite capacity, public and private, Adani Power's share is 7.9%.

How this was measured

Adani Power's capacity is its gross consolidated thermal capacity at 31 March from its annual reports, which counts subsidiaries in full. The denominators are the Central Electricity Authority's installed capacity reports for utilities at 31 March, which leave out captive plants. Adani Power's own share claims change basis between years (its 2024-25 figure leaves Godda out, its 2025-26 figure puts it in); this calculation includes Godda throughout from 2023-24. Whether the CEA counts Godda in Jharkhand's private capacity was not confirmed.
