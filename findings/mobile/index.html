Findings · Telecom · Part 1, Concentration

Jio holds 41% of India's mobile subscribers ten years after it launched, and the market is now highly concentrated

In March 2016, before Jio's launch, TRAI listed 12 mobile licensees with subscribers; in March 2026 it listed 5, Jio among them, the others having merged or stopped reporting. The concentration index rose from 1,544 to 3,255 over the same years. The 2023 United States Merger Guidelines treat a market above 1,800 as highly concentrated; Indian competition law sets no such threshold.

Jio and Airtel between them now have 77% of subscribers. The rest is mostly Vodafone Idea, itself the 2018 merger of two operators, and the state-owned BSNL.

Jio grew by building its own network. A complaint that its launch pricing was predatory went to the Competition Commission in 2017 (Case 03 of 2017); the order could not be retrieved for this paper.

How this was measured

Shares are of reported mobile subscribers at the end of March in TRAI's monthly press releases, each operator over TRAI's printed all-India total; in every year the operators add up to that total. From March 2026 TRAI counts machine-to-machine SIMs, most of them Airtel's, so 2025-26 is shown without them. Reported subscribers include inactive connections, and the active share differs by operator (in March 2025, 96% of Jio's and 63% of BSNL's subscribers were active on the peak day), so the shares overstate some operators. The index sums the squared shares of every licensee.
