Findings · Airports · Part 1, Concentration

The airports Adani runs carry 23% of India's air passengers, fewer than the 28% the same airports carried in 2013-14

Adani took over Ahmedabad, Lucknow and Mangaluru from October 2020, Mumbai on 13 July 2021, and Jaipur, Guwahati and Thiruvananthapuram later in 2021. Navi Mumbai opened in 2025-26. Since 2022-23, the first full year with all seven, the share has stayed between 22.7% and 23.5%.

The share fell from 28.1% to 23.2% before Adani ran any of them, because traffic grew faster at other airports. What changed after 2020 is who runs them. One company now operates airports handling close to a quarter of passenger movements, Mumbai's among them.

Six of these airports came from one privatisation round in 2019. The Ministry of Civil Aviation records 32 bids from 10 bidders, with the per-passenger fee as the bid, and Adani the highest bidder at all six. A group of secretaries decided on 17 November 2018 to set no cap on the number of airports one bidder could win, citing the six airports' 9.5% share of traffic. How the round was decided is the subject of Part 2.

How this was measured

Shares are of passenger movements at each airport in the Airports Authority of India's annual traffic tables, domestic and international together. AAI counts a passenger at both the airport they leave and the airport they reach, so the share is of airport throughput and does not count travellers. The same set of airports is used in every year so that the line follows the same airports through the change of owner. 2024-25 and 2025-26 are provisional.
