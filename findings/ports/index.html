Findings · Ports · Part 1, Concentration

Adani's ports handled 27% of India's port cargo in 2025-26, up from 12% in 2013-14

The share rose in 10 of the 12 years. The two steepest years were 2020-21 and 2021-22, when it went from 16.9% to 23.6%, the years Krishnapatnam, Dighi and Gangavaram joined the group.

Most of the rise came from buying ports other companies built and ran, starting with Dhamra in June 2014, Kattupalli in June 2018, Krishnapatnam in October 2020, Dighi in February 2021, Gangavaram in stages to April 2021, Karaikal in April 2023 and Gopalpur in October 2024. Mundra, the group's first port, remains its largest.

The company's own share claims match this calculation for the last three years. For 2020-21 the company says 25%; on the same official denominator its own cargo figure gives 19.8%.

How this was measured

The numerator is cargo at Adani Ports' Indian ports from its annual reports; from 2022-23 the company's headline figure also includes ports abroad, which are left out. The denominator is all cargo at major and non-major ports from the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways. Company and ministry figures for the same port differ a little (Mundra in 2023-24 handled 179.6 million tonnes in the company's report, 172.5 in the ministry's), so each side of the ratio comes from one source throughout. As a check from official figures alone, the non-major ports Adani ran in 2023-24 handled about 23% of the national total in the ministry's port-wise table; that leaves out Adani's terminals inside major ports, so it is a lower bound.
