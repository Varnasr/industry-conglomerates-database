Findings · Debt · Part 3, Finance and risk

By its own account, the Adani group replaced Indian bank loans with bonds and foreign banks, and its founders all but stopped borrowing against their shares

In the group's investor presentation, Indian public-sector banks provided 55% of long-term debt in March 2016 and 13% in March 2024; private Indian banks went from 31% to 2%. Bonds, foreign banks and non-bank lenders took their place. This is the group's claim; public-sector bank exposure has never been officially disclosed.

Pledged founders' shares tell the same story. In March 2020 the founders had pledged 58% of their Adani Ports holding; by March 2025 almost nothing. The group said its founders prepaid US$ 2.15 billion of share-backed loans in early 2023.

How this was measured

Lender shares are read from a chart in Adani Ports' March 2025 investor presentation and are a company claim. Pledges are from the annual reports' MGT-9 tables (to 2020) and NSE shareholding filings (from September 2021).
