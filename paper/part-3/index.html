Finance and risk: how the positions are paid for, and what the regulators and courts found

Part 3 looks at the money behind the assets: how much each company owes against what it earns, who lends, how much of the founders' stock is pledged, what happened in the weeks after January 2023, and what Indian and American regulators and courts have decided.

1. Debt against earnings

Net debt divided by a year's earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation says how many years of operating earnings it would take to repay what a company owes, net of its cash. The higher the number, the more the company depends on lenders continuing to lend.

The companies differ more than the group label suggests. Adani Power, at about 8 times in 2016-17, was down to 0.96 in 2023-24 and 1.93 in 2025-26. Adani Ports has stayed between 1.8 and 3.9 and ended at 1.82. Adani Green, building fast, has never been below six and ended at 8.32. Adani Enterprises, the group's incubator for airports, roads and new energy, rose back to 6.04. Reliance Industries has been below one since 2020-21.

A group-wide figure is not shown because the companies lend to and own parts of each other, and adding their debts would count some twice. The group's own portfolio figures use broader definitions of cash and earnings and come out lower.

2. Who lends

The only breakdown of who lends to the Adani companies comes from the group's own presentations, so it is the group's claim. By that account, Indian banks provided most of its long-term debt in 2016 and very little by 2024; bonds, foreign banks and non-bank lenders took their place.

How much public-sector banks lend to the group has never been officially disclosed. Asked in Parliament in 2023, the government and the Reserve Bank cited section 45E of the Reserve Bank of India Act, which bars disclosure of a borrower's credit information.

3. Pledged shares

When founders pledge their shares to borrow, a falling share price can force sales. In March 2020 the founders had pledged 58% of their holding in Adani Ports, 50% in Adani Enterprises and 54% in Adani Energy Solutions. By March 2025 these were near zero. The steepest fall came between December 2022 and March 2023, when the group said its founders had prepaid US$ 2.15 billion of share-backed loans.

4. January to March 2023

Hindenburg Research published its report on the group on 24 January 2023, as Adani Enterprises was preparing a follow-on share sale of up to Rs 20,000 crore. The sale closed fully subscribed and the board withdrew it on 1 February. By 1 March, the government told Parliament, the nine listed Adani companies had lost around 60% of their market value, while the Nifty 50 fell around 4.5%. On 10 February Moody's moved the outlook to negative on Adani Electricity Mumbai, Adani Green Energy, Adani Green Energy Restricted Group 1, Adani Transmission Step-One, citing the fall in equity values. On 2 March GQG Partners bought shares worth Rs 15,446 crore from the founders' family trust.

5. Public money

The Life Insurance Corporation, which is owned by the government, disclosed in January 2023 that it had bought Adani shares for Rs 30,127 crore, then worth Rs 56,142 crore. By 30 September 2025 its Adani equity and debt together stood at Rs 48,285 crore at book value, according to a reply in the Lok Sabha. For public-sector banks there is no equivalent disclosure.

6. The legal record

Three years of proceedings in India and the United States have produced holdings, settlements and allegations, and they should not be mixed up.

What courts and regulators decided

  • Supreme Court of India, 3 January 2024 (Vishal Tiwari v. Union of India, 2024 INSC 3). The Court declined to move the investigation to a special team or the CBI, upheld SEBI's rule changes, and said it had not interfered with the outcome of SEBI's investigations. It did not decide whether any Adani company broke the law. A review petition was dismissed in May 2024.
  • SEBI, 18 September 2025. Two orders found allegations about the Milestone, Rehvar and Adicorp transactions "not established", because the dealings were with parties that were not related under the definition in force before 2021.
  • SEBI, 28 September 2026. The order on the central question, whether offshore funds holding Adani shares were controlled by the founders' side, found the allegation "not established" for want of evidence that Vinod Adani directed their investments. The order adds that there was no allegation that he financed them, and that the result could have been different if there had been. Two individuals linked to the funds were fined Rs 20 lakh each for giving incorrect or incomplete information. The companies, Gautam Adani and 13 directors settled for Rs 1.48 crore without admitting anything.
  • SEBI settlements, September 2026. Five companies settled charges over undisclosed related-party dealings in 2012-13 and audits signed by firms without valid peer-review certificates; two executives settled a separate charge. None admitted the charges.
  • United States. The indictment unsealed in November 2024 charged Gautam Adani, Sagar Adani and Vneet Jaain with securities and wire fraud conspiracy and securities fraud, and five others connected with Azure Power and a Canadian pension fund with bribery conspiracy. On 10 August 2026, on the prosecutors' motion, the court dismissed the charges against the three Adani defendants with prejudice, stating that this expressed no opinion on the merits. The same day it entered a consent judgment in the SEC's civil case: Gautam Adani pays US$ 6 million and Sagar Adani US$ 12 million, without admitting or denying the allegations. The bribery charges against the five others remain, and the prosecutors have asked the appeals court to dismiss them.

What remains allegation

The Hindenburg report, the OCCRP's reporting and the indictment's account of a bribery scheme are allegations; no court has made a finding on them. The indictment alleges that offers of payment to Indian officials were made through the three Adani defendants; Adani Green has stated that they were not charged under the bribery statute, which is true of the counts. Both statements are reproduced so the reader can see the difference.

Bhatia's film

On 26 October 2026 the Calcutta High Court, in a defamation suit brought by Adani Enterprises, restrained publication of Sabeer Bhatia's video until 31 January 2027 and ordered platforms to take it down, without having heard Bhatia. The order says it does not restrain constructive criticism or fair reporting on the group or on government policy.

7. Reliance

Reliance declared itself net-debt-free on 19 June 2020. The claim compared its net debt of Rs 1,61,035 crore at 31 March 2020 with the capital it raised or agreed to raise afterwards, from selling stakes in Jio Platforms and a rights issue, and it uses a definition of cash that includes some long-term investments. The first year-end on which the balance sheet bore it out was March 2021. Net debt has since returned, to Rs 1,24,717 crore by the company's figure in March 2026, at well under one year's earnings.

In May 2026 the Supreme Court set aside SEBI's fraud finding and Rs 447 crore disgorgement against Reliance over trading in Reliance Petroleum shares in 2007, while upholding a penalty for a disclosure breach; SEBI's Adani order of September 2026 relies on that judgment. The KG-D6 gas-migration appeals are before the Supreme Court.

8. What it adds up to

The Adani companies are not one balance sheet. Adani Power and Adani Ports have cut their debt relative to earnings to levels lenders treat as comfortable; Adani Green and Adani Enterprises carry far more, because they are building. The group replaced Indian bank lending with bonds and foreign banks over the past decade, by its own account, and its founders all but stopped borrowing against their shares after January 2023.

On the legal side, the question at the centre of the Hindenburg report, whether offshore funds were fronts for the founders, has been decided in the group's favour on the evidence SEBI gathered, and SEBI said plainly what that evidence did not cover. The American criminal case against the Adanis is over without a finding either way. Neither outcome is a finding that the allegations were false, and neither is a finding that they were true. Where the law offers settlement without admission, the record will often end there.

What the record cannot show is the exposure of India's public-sector banks, because it has not been disclosed. A country that publishes which company bought each electoral bond, by court order, does not publish how much its own banks have lent to its largest borrowers. That gap is a policy choice, and closing it would answer more than this paper can.
